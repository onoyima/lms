## Setting a paper

Exams > New exam. Formats: objective (CBT), essay, mixed, practical (criteria with allowed score steps, several examiners, average) and viva (one overall criterion). Each section can draw *N* questions at random from a larger set. The paper goes to a second person for approval before it can be scheduled.

## Examiners for one viva, practical or defence

For viva, practical and research (defence) exams, **Examiners** > *Examiner accounts for this exam only*: enter the examiner's first name, last name, a username and a password, then give those to the examiner (often an external examiner).

- They sign in with the username and password and see only this exam's scoring sheet, plus the meeting room if the viva is held online. Every other page sends them back to the sheet.
- One computer at a time: a second sign-in is refused while the first is active. If they move computers or their browser closed, press **Reset session**; they can then sign in again anywhere. Signing out also frees the account.
- When the exam is over (its last sitting has ended, or the exam is closed or archived) the account stops working, including any page left open.
- **New password** sets another password; **Remove** stops the account at once. Scores already given stay.

## Rules and proctoring

On the **Rules & proctoring** tab choose which checks apply: tab and focus checks, full screen, webcam snapshots, face presence, microphone and **noise alerts**, screen sharing, a **second camera** (phone or USB), live video, recordings, identity checks, and whether the desktop app should **close screen-sharing and remote-control apps**. Allowed channels: browser, desktop exam app, Safe Exam Browser.

## Sittings and candidates

Add a sitting (hall or remote), then candidates: from course registration, from a parent exam (resits), or by pasting matriculation numbers (see the **Exam candidates** template). The timetable generator avoids clashes and respects each student's daily limit.

## Integrity review

After the exam, the **Integrity review** tab lists attempts whose pattern is statistically unusual for that exam — scored far above what the time taken predicts, hard questions answered correctly very fast, many identical wrong answers shared with one other candidate, most answers saved at the last minute, or a high proctoring score. Clear a flag, or open an incident so the full procedure applies. Marks are never changed here.
